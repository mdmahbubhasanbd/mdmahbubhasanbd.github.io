1. Microscopy and staining (MBG 1105, 1102)

Why it matters: The first step of identification is seeing the cell. Gram reaction splits almost all bacteria into two groups and guides every later test.

  • Resolution: the smallest distance at which two points can be told apart. About 0.2 micrometre for a light microscope with a 100x oil-immersion objective (numerical aperture about 1.25). Magnification without resolution is useless.
  • Types: bright-field, dark-field (live motile cells, spirochaetes), phase-contrast (living unstained cells), fluorescence (labelled antibodies or dyes), electron microscopy (TEM, SEM).
  • Gram stain: heat-fix, crystal violet 1 min, Gram's iodine 1 min, decolourise with alcohol/acetone 10 to 20 seconds, safranin 30 to 60 seconds. Gram-positive cells keep the violet (thick peptidoglycan), Gram-negative cells turn pink (thin peptidoglycan plus outer membrane). Pitfalls: over-decolourising makes Gram-positives look negative; old cultures also lose the stain.
  • Acid-fast (Ziehl-Neelsen): carbol fuchsin with heat, decolourise with acid-alcohol, counterstain with methylene blue. Mycobacteria stay red because of mycolic acids.
  • Spore stain (Schaeffer-Fulton): malachite green with steam, safranin counterstain. Spores green, vegetative cells pink.
  • Others: capsule (negative stain), flagella (special stains), simple stain, hanging drop for motility.

2. Culture media and pure-culture techniques (MBG 1105, 1106, 1201)

  • Media types: complex vs chemically defined; selective (inhibit unwanted organisms, e.g. MacConkey, Baird-Parker with tellurite and lithium chloride), differential (show a reaction, e.g. MacConkey lactose, blood agar haemolysis), enrichment (favour the target before plating), transport and anaerobic media.
  • Pure culture: streak plate, pour plate, spread plate. Streak with a flamed loop in a quadrant pattern to dilute cells until single colonies appear.
  • Serial dilution and counting: ten-fold dilutions in sterile diluent; plate 0.1 mL (spread) or 1 mL (pour). CFU per mL = colonies counted x dilution factor / volume plated. Count plates in the countable range (about 30 to 300 colonies, depending on the standard).
  • Miles and Misra: drop-plate method with 20 microlitre drops, six per dilution, counted where a drop has 2 to 20 colonies.
  • Preservation: slants at 4 degrees C (short term), glycerol stocks at -80 degrees C, freeze-drying (long term).

3. Sterilisation, disinfection and control of microbial growth (MBG 1105, 1203, 4203)

  • Moist heat (autoclave): 121 degrees C, about 15 psi (103 kPa), at least 15 minutes after the load reaches temperature. Kills spores because steam transfers heat efficiently.
  • Dry heat: 160 to 170 degrees C for 1 to 2 hours (glassware, powders).
  • Filtration: 0.22 micrometre membranes for heat-labile liquids; HEPA filters for air.
  • Radiation and gas: gamma or electron beam; ethylene oxide for heat-sensitive devices (needs aeration and residue testing).
  • Kinetics: the D-value is the time at a given temperature to kill 90 percent (one log). The z-value is the temperature change that changes D tenfold. F0 is the lethality expressed as equivalent minutes at 121 degrees C. Medical products target a sterility assurance level (SAL) of 10^-6.
  • Monitoring: physical (time, temperature, pressure), chemical indicators (colour change, show exposure only), biological indicators (spores of Geobacillus stearothermophilus for steam, Bacillus atrophaeus for dry heat and ethylene oxide; show real kill). Always combine the three.
  • Disinfectants: alcohols (70 percent works better than 100 percent), chlorine, hydrogen peroxide, quaternary ammonium compounds, phenolics. Activity depends on concentration, contact time, organic load and temperature.

4. Bacterial growth and its measurement (MBG 1201)

  • Phases: lag, exponential (log), stationary, death. Generation time = time for the population to double; N = N0 x 2^n.
  • Direct methods: viable plate count, total count in a counting chamber (Neubauer), microscopy.
  • Indirect methods: turbidity (optical density at 600 nm), dry weight, protein, metabolic activity.
  • Culture modes: batch, fed-batch, continuous (chemostat).
  • Physical factors: temperature (psychrophile, mesophile, thermophile), pH, oxygen (aerobe, anaerobe, facultative, microaerophile), osmotic pressure and water activity.

5. Biochemical identification of bacteria (MBG 2102, 2106)

  • Catalase (bubbles with H2O2: staphylococci positive, streptococci negative); oxidase (Pseudomonas positive, Enterobacteriaceae negative).
  • IMViC: indole, methyl red, Voges-Proskauer, citrate. Separates E. coli (++--) from Klebsiella/Enterobacter (--++).
  • TSI/KIA agar: reads glucose, lactose/sucrose fermentation, gas and H2S in one tube (slant/butt colours).
  • Urease, motility (MIU), nitrate reduction, hydrolysis of starch, gelatin, casein, lipid, litmus milk.
  • Coagulase separates Staphylococcus aureus (positive) from coagulase-negative staphylococci. Mannitol salt agar is selective for staphylococci.
  • Final identification uses a scheme such as Bergey's Manual, API strips, MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry or 16S rRNA sequencing.

6. Antibiotics, susceptibility testing and resistance (MBG 1203, 2102, 2205)

  • Classes and targets: beta-lactams (cell wall: penicillin, cephalosporins), aminoglycosides and tetracyclines (30S ribosome), macrolides and chloramphenicol (50S), quinolones (DNA gyrase), polymyxins (membrane).
  • Disk diffusion (Kirby-Bauer): Mueller-Hinton agar, inoculum 0.5 McFarland (about 1.5 x 10^8 CFU/mL) spread as a lawn, disks applied within 15 minutes, incubate 35 degrees C for 16 to 18 hours, measure the zone in mm and read it against CLSI or EUCAST breakpoints as susceptible, intermediate or resistant.
  • MIC and MBC: MIC is the lowest concentration that stops visible growth (broth microdilution, final inoculum about 5 x 10^5 CFU/mL, 16 to 20 hours); MBC is the lowest concentration that kills at least 99.9 percent.
  • Resistance mechanisms: enzymatic inactivation (beta-lactamases), altered target (PBP2a from mecA in MRSA), efflux pumps, reduced permeability. Resistance spreads through plasmids, transposons and integrons.
  • MRSA: today screened with a cefoxitin 30 microgram disk (for S. aureus a zone of 21 mm or less means MRSA) or oxacillin, and confirmed by mecA PCR or PBP2a latex test.

7. Food microbiology: spoilage, preservation and pathogens (MBG 3107, 3108)

  • Intrinsic factors: pH, water activity (aw), redox potential, nutrients, natural antimicrobials, structure. Extrinsic factors: storage temperature, humidity, gas atmosphere.
  • Preservation principles: heat (pasteurisation, sterilisation), cold (refrigeration, freezing), drying and low aw, acid and fermentation, preservatives (sorbate, benzoate, nitrite), irradiation, modified atmosphere packaging, and hurdle technology (several mild barriers together).
  • Typical pathogens: Salmonella (poultry, eggs), Listeria monocytogenes (cold-growing, ready-to-eat foods), E. coli O157 and STEC (beef, produce), Campylobacter (poultry), Staphylococcus aureus (heat-stable toxin, handling), Bacillus cereus (rice, spores), Clostridium botulinum and C. perfringens, Vibrio (seafood).
  • Indicator organisms: total aerobic count, Enterobacteriaceae, coliforms, E. coli, enterococci. They show hygiene status, not necessarily the presence of pathogens.
  • Standard methods (ISO series): total viable count ISO 4833-1 (30 degrees C, 72 h), Enterobacteriaceae ISO 21528, E. coli ISO 16649, coagulase-positive staphylococci ISO 6888, Salmonella ISO 6579-1 (pre-enrichment in buffered peptone water, selective enrichment RVS and MKTTn, plating on XLD and a second agar), Listeria ISO 11290, B. cereus ISO 7932, yeasts and moulds ISO 21527. Media quality control follows ISO 11133.
  • Fermented foods: yoghurt, cheese, sauerkraut, kimchi, tempeh, miso, bread. Lactic acid bacteria acidify the food and protect it.
  • Methylene blue reduction test: a quick milk quality test; the faster the dye loses colour, the higher the bacterial load.

8. Quality control of food and beverages, HACCP and sanitation (MBG 4205, 4206)

  • HACCP (Codex): 5 preliminary steps (team, product description, intended use, flow diagram, on-site confirmation) and 7 principles: hazard analysis; critical control points (CCP); critical limits; monitoring; corrective actions; verification; documentation. Prerequisite programmes (GMP, GHP, cleaning, pest control, supplier control, training) sit underneath.
  • Sampling plans: attribute plans (n, c, m, M) from ICMSF; the more hazardous the organism, the stricter the plan.
  • Water testing: multiple-tube MPN or membrane filtration; indicators E. coli, coliforms, enterococci. Drinking water should contain no E. coli in 100 mL.
  • Sanitation: clean first, then disinfect; verify with ATP bioluminescence, swabs, contact plates.
  • Systems: ISO 22000, FSSC 22000, IFS Food, BRCGS Food Safety are built on HACCP plus management-system requirements.
  • Fish and seafood quality: sensory scoring, histamine, TVB-N, microbial counts.

9. Pharmaceutical and medical-device microbiology (MBG 4203, 4204)

  • Sterility test (Ph. Eur. 2.6.1, USP <71>): membrane filtration (preferred) or direct inoculation into fluid thioglycollate medium (30 to 35 degrees C, bacteria) and soybean-casein digest broth (20 to 25 degrees C, fungi), 14 days, with growth promotion tests and a method suitability test (shows the product does not inhibit growth).
  • Bacterial endotoxin test (LAL, Ph. Eur. 2.6.14, USP <85>): Gram-negative lipopolysaccharide activates the Limulus lysate cascade. Three techniques: gel-clot (clot present or absent at a labelled sensitivity), kinetic turbidimetric and chromogenic (reaction time or colour measured in a plate reader). Controls: negative water control, standard curve with control standard endotoxin, positive product control (spike recovery must be 50 to 200 percent). Samples are diluted but not beyond the maximum valid dilution (MVD). Typical medical-device limit: 20 EU per device (0.5 EU/mL in extract). Extraction uses endotoxin-free water; glassware is depyrogenated at 250 degrees C.
  • Pyrogen tests: rabbit pyrogen test (historic), monocyte activation test (for non-endotoxin pyrogens).
  • Bioburden: count of viable organisms on a product before sterilisation (ISO 11737-1). It sets the sterilisation dose. Sterility testing of devices: ISO 11737-2.
  • Sterilisation standards: moist heat ISO 17665, ethylene oxide ISO 11135, radiation ISO 11137, biological indicators ISO 11138, chemical indicators ISO 11140.
  • Cleanrooms and environmental monitoring: ISO 14644 classes; EU GMP grades A to D; settle plates, contact plates, active air sampling, glove prints; alert and action limits; trending.
  • Aseptic technique: laminar flow cabinet, gowning, disinfection, no cross-traffic.
  • Preservative efficacy, MIC/MBC and antibiotic potency assays (cylinder-plate or turbidimetric).
  • Vaccines and immunological products: potency, sterility and safety testing.

10. Microbiology of frozen foods (MBG 4109)

  • Freezing at -18 degrees C or colder stops growth, but does not sterilise: many organisms survive and some pathogens (Listeria, Salmonella, S. aureus, noroviruses) survive for months.
  • Freeze injury: ice crystals and osmotic stress damage cells; injured cells need a resuscitation step before selective plating.
  • Thawing is the risky step: growth resumes quickly, especially in fish and meat.
  • Fish: spoilage by Pseudomonas and Shewanella; histamine in scombroid fish; Vibrio, Listeria and C. botulinum type E as hazards. Good practice: fast freezing, constant -18 degrees C, short thawing, hygiene in the plant.
  • Plant design matters: hygienic zoning, controlled internal environment, cleaning schedules.

11. Molecular genetics and genetic engineering (MBG 2203, 3105, 4103)

  • DNA isolation: lyse cells, remove protein and RNA, precipitate DNA with ethanol or use silica columns. Check with agarose gel electrophoresis (DNA is negatively charged and runs to the anode; ethidium bromide or safer dyes show bands).
  • PCR: denaturation 94 to 95 degrees C, annealing about 50 to 65 degrees C (about 5 degrees below the primer Tm), extension 72 degrees C (about 1 minute per kb), 25 to 35 cycles. Needs template, two primers, dNTPs, Taq polymerase, buffer with Mg2+. Always run a negative and a positive control.
  • qPCR / real-time PCR: fluorescence measured each cycle; the Ct value is the cycle where the signal crosses the threshold (lower Ct = more template). RT-PCR first copies RNA into cDNA.
  • Cloning: cut vector and insert with restriction enzymes, join with ligase, transform E. coli, select on antibiotic plates (blue-white screening with lacZ).
  • Gene transfer in bacteria: transformation, conjugation, transduction. Plasmids, transposons, integrons carry resistance genes.
  • Operons: lac operon (induction, catabolite repression) and trp operon (repression and attenuation).
  • Blotting: Southern (DNA), Northern (RNA), Western (protein).

12. Immunology and diagnostic microbiology (MBG 3101, 4105, 4111)

  • Innate vs adaptive immunity; antibody classes IgG, IgM, IgA, IgE, IgD; antigen, epitope, hapten; MHC class I and II; complement.
  • Hypersensitivity types I to IV; vaccines (live attenuated, inactivated, subunit, conjugate, DNA).
  • Serology: agglutination, Widal test (typhoid), ASO titre, VDRL, immunodiffusion, complement fixation.
  • ELISA: direct, indirect, sandwich and competitive formats; an enzyme-linked antibody converts a substrate to colour that is read at a set wavelength; compare with cut-off from controls.
  • Immunoblot, immunofluorescence, RIA.
  • Molecular diagnostics: PCR, real-time PCR, nucleic-acid probes, sequencing.

13. Analytical techniques (MBG 4107, 4108)

  • Spectrophotometry: Beer-Lambert law A = epsilon x c x l. Build a calibration curve, never read outside its linear range.
  • Centrifugation: differential and density-gradient; relative centrifugal force depends on radius and speed.
  • Chromatography: paper and thin-layer (Rf value), column, ion-exchange, size-exclusion, affinity, GC, HPLC.
  • Electrophoresis: agarose for DNA; SDS-PAGE for proteins (SDS gives a uniform negative charge, so proteins separate by size; stacking gel pH 6.8, resolving gel pH 8.8; Coomassie or silver stain).
  • Protein characterisation: molecular weight, amino-acid composition, Edman sequencing, mass spectrometry; Sanger reagent FDNB labels the N-terminus.
  • Others: NMR and mass spectrometry, biosensors, radioisotope techniques, cell culture.

14. Environmental microbiology and bioremediation (MBG 2103, 4201)

  • Water quality: BOD (oxygen consumed in 5 days at 20 degrees C), COD, TOC, nitrogen and phosphorus; indicator bacteria.
  • Wastewater treatment: primary (settling), secondary (activated sludge, trickling filter, anaerobic digestion), tertiary (disinfection, nutrient removal).
  • Water treatment: coagulation, flocculation, sedimentation, filtration, chlorination, ozone, UV.
  • Bioremediation: biostimulation and bioaugmentation; degradation of hydrocarbons, pesticides, dyes; biodeterioration of materials.
  • Public health in Bangladesh: safe water supply, tube-well coverage, sanitation.

15. Industrial microbiology and fermentation technology (MBG 3111, 4101, 4207)

  • Products: ethanol, organic acids (citric, lactic, acetic), amino acids, enzymes, antibiotics, vaccines, single-cell protein, baker's yeast, beer, wine, yoghurt, biogas.
  • Process steps: strain selection and improvement, inoculum build-up, medium design, sterilisation of medium and air, fermentation (batch, fed-batch, continuous), monitoring of temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen, foam, then downstream processing (separation, concentration, purification).
  • Bioreactors: stirred tank with impeller, baffles, sparger; scale-up keeps constant oxygen transfer (kLa) or power per volume.
  • Immobilised enzymes and cells (calcium alginate beads) allow reuse.

16. Genomics and bioinformatics (MBG 4209, 4210)

  • BLAST searches a sequence against databases to find similar sequences (E-value, identity, coverage).
  • Multiple sequence alignment (ClustalW) and phylogenetic trees (neighbour-joining, maximum likelihood) show relationships; bootstrap values show support.
  • 16S rRNA sequencing identifies bacteria; primer design needs correct Tm, GC content 40 to 60 percent, no self-dimers.
  • Protein tools: physicochemical parameters, subcellular localisation, secondary structure, homology modelling, structure quality (Ramachandran plot), active-site prediction, docking.
  • Sequencing: Sanger and next-generation sequencing, genome assembly and annotation.

17. Biostatistics (MBG 2209)

  • Descriptive: mean, median, standard deviation, standard error, confidence interval.
  • Tests: t-test (two means), ANOVA (three or more means, then post-hoc such as Tukey), chi-square (frequencies), correlation and regression, non-parametric tests when data are not normal.
  • p-value is the probability of seeing a result at least this extreme if the null hypothesis were true; type I error is a false positive, type II a false negative; power depends on sample size.
  • Epidemiology measures: incidence, prevalence, relative risk, odds ratio, survival analysis.

18. Other courses (short summaries)

  • Introductory microbiology: bacteria, archaea, viruses, fungi, algae, protozoa; Koch's postulates; scope.
  • Microbial ecology and extremophiles: communities, biological interactions, abiotic limits.
  • Microbial metabolism: glycolysis (EMP), TCA cycle, electron transport, ED and pentose-phosphate pathways, fermentations; anabolism, nitrogen fixation, autotrophy.
  • Medical, bacterial pathogenesis, virology, mycology, parasitology: virulence factors, toxins, host defences, major pathogens, viral replication and bacteriophages, fungi and parasites.
  • Enzymology: Michaelis-Menten kinetics, Km and Vmax, inhibition types.
  • Algology, soil and agricultural microbiology, biotechnology.

Practical skills practised in the B.Sc. laboratories

Media preparation and sterilisation; streak, pour and spread plating; serial dilution, Miles and Misra; Gram, acid-fast, capsule, spore and flagella staining; biochemical tests (IMViC, TSI, catalase, oxidase, urease); antibiotic sensitivity; milk quality (plate count, methylene blue); water analysis (MPN, membrane filtration); plasmid and genomic DNA isolation, agarose gel electrophoresis, transformation, restriction digestion, PCR (including HBV genes); SDS-PAGE and immunoblotting; ELISA, Widal, ASO, VDRL, haemagglutination; enzyme assays; whole-cell immobilisation; fermentation (bread, yoghurt, acetic and citric acid); thin-layer and paper chromatography; BLAST, ClustalW, phylogenetic tree, primer design and homology modelling.

M.Sc. Microbiology: important topics, explained

Noakhali Science and Technology University (NSTU), 2022 to 2023, 32 credits, non-thesis group (Group A: six theory courses, three practicals, project, laboratory training, critical review, seminar, viva). CGPA 3.63 of 4.00, second of seven. The syllabus is the department's postgraduate syllabus (course codes MBPG 1101 to MBPG 1121). This file keeps the most useful topics in plain language so they can be revised quickly.

1. Management and control of microbiological safety of foods and pharmaceuticals (MBPG 1111)

This course is the closest match to food quality assurance work.

  • Microbiological criteria: a criterion says which organism, which limits and which sampling plan decide whether a lot is acceptable. Codex and ICMSF principles apply: criteria must be needed, achievable and based on risk.
  • Sampling plans (ICMSF): attribute plans count samples that exceed a limit. Two-class plan: n samples, at most c may exceed the limit m. Three-class plan: n, c, m and an upper limit M that no sample may exceed. The more severe the hazard, the larger n and the smaller c. Variable plans use the measured values and assume a (log-)normal distribution.
  • Microbiological risk assessment (Codex four steps): hazard identification, hazard characterisation (dose-response), exposure assessment, risk characterisation. Risk management and risk communication follow. Typical case studies: Salmonella in dried milk, Listeria monocytogenes in sausage, E. coli in frozen ground beef, aflatoxin in peanuts.
  • Outbreak investigation: confirm the outbreak, define cases, find a common source with an epidemiological study (case-control or cohort), test food and clinical samples, trace back, apply control measures, report.
  • Regulation and trade: Codex Alimentarius, ICMSF, WHO, WTO agreements on sanitary and phytosanitary measures (SPS) and technical barriers to trade (TBT); national authorities such as BSTI (Bangladesh) and FDA.
  • Quality assurance: GMP, in-process control, quality control, validation, monitoring. This is the same logic as the HACCP and quality management modules of the Kassel and Fulda master.

2. Molecular epidemiology and biostatistics (MBPG 1109)

  • Epidemiology basics: determinants of disease, incidence and prevalence, types of studies (descriptive, case-control, cohort, cross-sectional, trial), epidemic vs endemic vs sporadic.
  • Infection control: surveillance, outbreak detection, role of the laboratory, hospital-acquired infection.
  • Typing systems: phenotypic (biotyping, antibiogram, serotyping, phage typing, MLEE) and genotypic (plasmid profile, restriction analysis, RFLP and Southern blot, pulsed-field gel electrophoresis (PFGE), PCR-based typing, sequencing). A good typing method is typeable, reproducible, discriminatory and easy to use.
  • Statistics: correlation and regression, experimental design, SPSS.

3. Immunopathology and vaccine development (MBPG 1105)

  • Antibody-mediated damage: neutralising antibodies, cytotoxic and cytolytic reactions, immune-complex and granulomatous reactions.
  • Antibody engineering: cloning antibody genes, recombinant antibodies, humanisation, therapeutic uses.
  • Vaccine types: killed, live attenuated, subunit, conjugate, DNA, edible vaccines; adjuvants; vaccination programmes. Candidate targets: anthrax, botulism, cholera, typhoid, hepatitis, malaria, cancer.
  • Toxicology basics: dose-response, LD50, metabolism of toxic substances, environmental toxicology.

4. Enzyme and protein biotechnology (MBPG 1107)

  • Enzyme production: strain choice, fermentation, downstream processing (cell disruption, precipitation, chromatography, formulation).
  • Overproduction: expression in bacteria, yeast, baculovirus; fusion tags; inclusion bodies and refolding.
  • Industrial uses: food (amylases, proteases, lactase, pectinases), detergents, leather, textile, paper, starch processing, diagnostics.
  • Protein engineering and stability: site-directed mutagenesis, directed evolution, denaturation and renaturation, immobilisation.
  • Protein characterisation: SDS-PAGE, isoelectric focusing, Western blotting, mass spectrometry, amino-acid sequencing.

5. Advanced molecular genetics (MBPG 1103)

  • Gene regulation in eukaryotes: transcription factors, enhancers, alternative splicing, mRNA stability, DNA methylation and imprinting.
  • Studying gene function: reporter genes, DNA-protein binding assays, hybrid-arrest translation, yeast two-hybrid and phage display for protein-protein interaction.
  • Gene therapy and genetic disorders: somatic and germ-line therapy, antisense RNA and ribozymes, cystic fibrosis, Huntington's disease.
  • Cell signalling: G-protein-linked receptors (cAMP, inositol phosphates, calcium), receptor tyrosine kinases and the Ras-MAP kinase cascade, JAK-STAT, TGF-beta and Smad.

6. Molecular oncology and virology (MBPG 1115)

  • Cancer biology: cell cycle checkpoints, oncogenes and tumour suppressors (Ras, Myc, p53, Rb), angiogenesis, metastasis, chemical and physical carcinogens.
  • Oncogenic viruses: HPV, EBV, HBV, HTLV-1; viral oncogenes and transformation of DNA and RNA viruses.
  • Viral pathogenesis and persistence: latency of herpesviruses and HIV, virulence determinants, virus evolution and emerging viruses (dengue, Japanese encephalitis, Ebola).
  • Therapy: antibody therapy, virotherapy, immunotherapy.

7. Pharmaceutical biotechnology (MBPG 1119)

  • Drug development: discovery, genomics and proteomics, pre-clinical and clinical phases, approval.
  • Biopharmaceutical production: recombinant DNA, animal cell culture, hybridoma technology (monoclonal antibodies).
  • Biological drugs: insulin, growth hormone, erythropoietin, interferons, interleukins, tPA, therapeutic enzymes, nucleic-acid therapies.
  • Antimicrobial peptides: structure, selective toxicity, membrane permeabilisation (barrel-stave, toroidal-pore and carpet models), resistance and toxicity problems.

8. Environmental biotechnology (MBPG 1101)

  • Risk assessment: environmental and health risk, microbiological risk assessment, persistence of transgenes.
  • Viable but non-culturable (VBNC) cells: cells that are alive but do not grow on routine media; detected by direct viable count, fluorescence or molecular methods. Important for water and food testing because plate counts underestimate them.
  • Bioremediation of xenobiotics: degradative genes and plasmids, community analysis, genetic fingerprinting.
  • Biological treatment: anaerobic digestion, activated sludge with nitrification and denitrification, trickling filters, lagoons, oxidation ditch.
  • Biomining and valorisation of organic waste: metals, enzymes, organic acids, bioplastics, feed.

9. Biomass and biofuel (MBPG 1113) and the critical review

  • Routes: biogas (methane) from waste, bioethanol from molasses or cellulose (yeast, Zymomonas mobilis), biohydrogen, biodiesel from crops and microalgae, microbial fuel cells.
  • Critical review written for this degree: "Microalgae as an alternative resource for bioenergy: an encouraging and cost-effective material for use in microbial fuel cells" (course MBPG 1112, submitted 15 September 2023). It covers microalgae history and definition, photoautotrophic, heterotrophic, mixotrophic and photoheterotrophic growth, open ponds and closed photobioreactors, genetic engineering, biodiesel, bioethanol, biohydrogen, methane, and microalgae at the anode and at the cathode of a microbial fuel cell (electricity generation plus CO2 capture).

10. Extremophiles and novel biological products (MBPG 1117)

  • Groups: thermophiles and hyperthermophiles, psychrophiles, halophiles, acidophiles, alkaliphiles, piezophiles, anaerobic extremophiles.
  • Adaptations: heat-stable proteins, membrane lipid changes, compatible solutes, DNA repair.
  • Extremozymes: Taq polymerase (from Thermus aquaticus) is the best-known example; amylases, cellulases, xylanases, proteases for industry.
  • Space microbiology: life-detection methods, survival of microbes in space.

11. Research methodology and scientific writing (MBPG 1121)

  • Research process: problem, literature review, objectives and hypotheses, materials and methods, results, discussion, conclusion.
  • Writing: proposal parts, manuscript structure (title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, references), plagiarism, ethics, time planning, critical review writing.

12. Practical courses and project (Group A)

  • General practicals (MBPG 1102): culture of aerobic and anaerobic bacteria, bacteriophage isolation and counting, TMV isolation, virus culture in embryonated eggs, growth curve, enzyme purification and PAGE, plasmid isolation, Agrobacterium gene transfer, cell and tissue culture, column chromatography.
  • Practical II (MBPG 1104): BOD and COD of water, alkalinity, chlorine, BSTI drinking-water standards, soil and seed pathogens, enzyme activity versus substrate, pH, temperature and salt, enzyme purification with ammonium sulphate, MIC, MBC and MFC, LD50, antibiotic and vitamin assays, serology (agglutination, immunodiffusion, haemagglutination, ELISA, complement fixation, VDRL).
  • Practical III (MBPG 1106): microbiological analysis of foods (isolation of E. coli, Salmonella, Shigella, Streptococcus, Staphylococcus), milk and dairy testing, yoghurt, clinical specimens (stool, urine, blood, throat), bacterial toxins, contamination testing of pharmaceutical products, vaccine and antibiotic production.
  • Project and laboratory training (MBPG 1108): Isolation and Characterization of Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) from Eggs of Avian Species, done in the department laboratory and in a medical-device manufacturer's QC laboratory.

How the MRSA egg project worked (a worked example of the standard method)

  1. Sampling: 50 samples (100 eggs, two per sample): duck, quail and poultry-farm eggs from local markets. Samples were taken to the laboratory in sterile bags.
  2. Shell and content preparation: shell rinse in phosphate-buffered saline with 15 minutes of agitation; content taken aseptically after cleaning the shell, soaking in 70 percent alcohol for 10 minutes and flaming, then homogenised.
  3. Enrichment: Staphylococci broth, 20 hours at 35 degrees C.
  4. Selective plating: streak onto Baird-Parker agar (tellurite and egg-yolk; S. aureus gives black shiny colonies with a clear zone), 24 hours at 37 degrees C.
  5. Identification: colony morphology and Gram stain (Gram-positive cocci in clusters).
  6. Methicillin susceptibility: disk diffusion on Mueller-Hinton agar following CLSI practice.
  7. Result: S. aureus on shells in 50 percent (duck), 60 percent (quail) and 80 percent (poultry-farm) of samples; in contents 70, 75 and 85 percent; 13 resistant and 16 intermediate isolates among the positives. Contents were contaminated more often than shells, which shows the egg can be a carrier despite its shell.
  8. Limits to remember: no coagulase or mecA PCR confirmation was done in this project, so the result shows methicillin-resistant phenotypes only; modern practice uses cefoxitin or oxacillin disks plus mecA or PBP2a confirmation.

M.Sc. International Food Business and Consumer Studies: important topics, explained

Joint degree of the University of Kassel (Organic Agricultural Sciences) and Hochschule Fulda (Nutritional, Food and Consumer Sciences), 120 ECTS, taught in English, started 10/2024. 1. Quality management in the food business (module F38)

  • Food safety and HACCP. Codex HACCP has 5 preparatory steps and 7 principles: hazard analysis, critical control points (CCP), critical limits, monitoring, corrective actions, verification, documentation. A CCP is a step where control is essential and a hazard can be prevented, removed or reduced to an acceptable level (for example a kill step or metal detection). Prerequisite programmes (GMP, GHP, cleaning, pest control, supplier approval, training, allergen management) control the general conditions.
  • Hazards: biological, chemical (including allergens), physical, plus food fraud and food defence in modern standards.
  • ISO 9001:2015: process approach, PDCA cycle, risk-based thinking, context of the organisation, leadership, documented information, internal audit, management review, continual improvement. Seven quality-management principles: customer focus, leadership, engagement of people, process approach, improvement, evidence-based decisions, relationship management.
  • Food standards recognised by GFSI: IFS Food, BRCGS Food Safety, FSSC 22000 (ISO 22000 plus prerequisite programme specifications). They add product safety culture, supplier control, traceability and auditing rules. Studied as knowledge; not a certification.
  • Statistical process control: control charts (X-bar and R, p charts), centre line and limits at about 3 standard deviations, rules for out-of-control signals, process capability indices Cp and Cpk.
  • Traceability: one step back, one step forward (Regulation (EC) 178/2002, Article 18). Test with a mock recall: time to find all affected lots.
  • Crisis management: crisis team, communication plan, withdrawal and recall procedures, authority notification (RASFF).
  • Quality tools: flow chart, Pareto, cause-and-effect (Ishikawa), 5 Whys, FMEA, audit checklists.

2. International legislation on consumer protection and food (module F31)

How it was assessed: oral exam; no written paper was submitted, so this section only describes what the module covers.

  • EU institutions: European Commission, Parliament and Council; European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) gives scientific advice, the Commission and member states manage risk.
  • General Food Law (EC) 178/2002: principles (risk analysis, precaution, transparency), Article 14 (food must not be unsafe), Article 17 (operators' responsibility), Article 18 (traceability), Articles 19 and 20 (withdrawal, recall), RASFF alert system.
  • Hygiene package: (EC) 852/2004 (all food businesses, HACCP principles), 853/2004 (products of animal origin), official controls (EU) 2017/625.
  • Labelling: (EU) 1169/2011 Food Information to Consumers: mandatory particulars (name, ingredient list, 14 allergens emphasised, net quantity, date marking, storage, business operator, origin where required, nutrition declaration per 100 g or 100 mL), minimum font size (x-height 1.2 mm), clarity and no misleading information.
  • Claims: (EC) 1924/2006 nutrition and health claims, only authorised claims; Nutri-Score is voluntary.
  • Novel and special foods: Novel Foods (EU) 2015/2283, GM food and feed (EC) 1829/2003 with labelling above 0.9 percent, functional and fortified foods.
  • Organic: (EU) 2018/848.
  • Risk analysis: risk assessment (science), risk management (policy), risk communication (dialogue).
  • Free movement of goods: mutual recognition, technical barriers to trade; consumer protection policy in the EU.

3. Food product development (module F37)

  • Stages: idea generation, screening, concept development and testing, formulation and prototype, consumer testing, shelf-life, labelling and legal check, scale-up, launch. A stage-gate process puts a decision between stages.
  • Idea generation: structured brainwriting (6-3-5: 6 people, 3 ideas, 5 minutes per round), SCAMPER, trend and gap analysis; idea evaluation with scoring and voting.
  • Formulation and food chemistry: water activity, pH, emulsifiers, hydrocolloids, sugar and fat reduction; ingredients must meet functional and legal rules; allergen cross-contact.
  • Nutrition calculation: food-composition software (for example PRODI) gives energy, macronutrients, fibre and nutrition score per portion.
  • Sensory and consumer testing: hedonic scale (liking), just-about-right (JAR) scales with penalty analysis (how much a deviation from ideal costs in liking), purchase intention, price range, label preference.
  • Shelf-life: microbiological, chemical (oxidation) and sensory limits; real-time and accelerated shelf-life testing (temperature increase; Q10 or Arrhenius model).
  • Project work with a partner: muesli-based bar for children for an organic farm, with idea generation, lab prototypes, allergen check, sensory test of 25 participants.

4. Innovative product development (elective, F-module)

  • Sustainable product development: raw material sourcing, upcycling of surplus (for example surplus tomatoes turned into powder), packaging, carbon and water footprint, regional supply.
  • Sensory methods: discrimination tests (triangle, duo-trio), descriptive analysis, affective tests; panel selection and training; software for sensory sessions and data (for example RedJade).
  • Consumer research software: survey and sensory data capture, statistics in SPSS or Excel.
  • Project: a fried chickpea snack with tomato powder ("Tomato Air Crunch Ball") with lab trials (drying, binding with egg, deep-frying beat baking), PRODI nutrition score B, and a consumer test with penalty analysis.

5. Food processing (bridging module)

How it was assessed: oral exam; no written paper was submitted, so this section only describes what the module covers.

  • Thermal processing: pasteurisation (milk HTST 72 degrees C for 15 s, LTLT 63 degrees C for 30 min), UHT (135 to 140 degrees C for a few seconds), retorting of canned food (F-value, commercial sterility), blanching. Heat load vs nutrient and flavour loss.
  • Dairy: standardisation, homogenisation, pasteurisation, cheese (coagulation, curd handling, salting, ripening), yoghurt (starter cultures), butter.
  • Meat and fish: slaughter hygiene, chilling, curing (nitrite and colour), smoking, fermentation (sausage), freezing; fish freshness indicators.
  • Eggs, fats and oils: egg products, refining of vegetable oils (degumming, neutralising, bleaching, deodorising), oxidation and rancidity.
  • Plant foods: cereals (milling, baking), legumes, fruit and vegetables (drying, juice, preserves), sugar, cocoa and chocolate, coffee and tea, beer, wine and spirits.
  • Quality and authenticity methods: chromatography, spectroscopy, DNA-based species identification, isotope ratio and NMR for origin, ELISA for allergens and adulteration.

6. Trends in food sciences and sustainable consumption (module F39)

  • Novel processing: high-pressure processing (400 to 600 MPa), pulsed electric fields, ultrasound, cold plasma, microwave and ohmic heating.
  • Alternative proteins: plant-based meat, dairy and seafood alternatives, fermentation-derived proteins, cultivated meat, insects; sensory, nutritional and acceptance questions.
  • Sustainability: life-cycle assessment (LCA), planetary boundaries, SDGs, food waste, packaging.
  • Nutrition trends: personalised nutrition, functional foods, sugar and salt reduction.
  • Method: research question, structured literature search, critical evaluation, presentation.

7. Consumer behaviour and consumer science (module F30)

  • Decision process: need recognition, information search, evaluation of alternatives, purchase, post-purchase evaluation (Engel-Kollat-Blackwell and Howard-Sheth models).
  • Psychological factors: needs and motivation (Maslow), perception, learning, attitudes (Fishbein multi-attribute model, Theory of Planned Behaviour), involvement, motivational conflicts (approach-approach, approach-avoidance, avoidance-avoidance).
  • Social and cultural factors: culture and Hofstede dimensions, reference groups, family decision making, social class, situational influences (store atmosphere, time pressure, promotions).
  • Segmentation: VALS (values and lifestyles, eight types) and the SINUS milieus.
  • Resources and trends: income and household expenditure data (for example from Eurostat), consumer knowledge, future trends.
  • Methods: surveys, observation, focus groups, interviews, mixed methods; AI tools such as ChatGPT can support trend search but need prompts and checking.
  • Exercises and final paper: twelve group exercises (for example breakfast culture comparison, family influence, consumer resources, attitudes to packaging, AI-driven consumer research, situational influences) and a final term paper on competencies built, including the "Go Zero" packaging project.

8. Research methods in social sciences (module F16) and applied research

How it was assessed: the methods module (F16) was a written exam (no paper to submit); the applied research project (F22) was a written report with a poster.

  • Philosophy: ontology, epistemology, methodology; deductive vs inductive research.
  • Qualitative: interviews, focus groups, content analysis, coding.
  • Quantitative: sampling (random, stratified, quota), questionnaire design (clear, neutral, one question one idea; Dillman's tailored design), scales (nominal, ordinal, interval, Likert), reliability (Cronbach's alpha) and validity.
  • Statistics: descriptive statistics, hypothesis testing, t-test, ANOVA, chi-square, correlation, multiple regression, factor and cluster analysis; software SPSS, Excel and Python tools.
  • Applied research project (F22, 18 ECTS): planning and documentation of a project with a company partner, project management, intercultural communication, scientific writing. Own project: AI in idea generation for seafood alternatives (survey with 73 valid responses, ANOVA, post-hoc tests).

9. Digital transformation in the food sector

  • Technologies: ERP and MES systems, IoT sensors and cold-chain monitoring, blockchain for traceability, big data and AI for demand forecasting and quality control, image analysis, e-commerce, precision agriculture.
  • Standards and law: GS1 identifiers and barcodes (GTIN, GLN, SSCC) for traceability, EU rules on traceability and official controls, RASFF.
  • Risks: data quality and standards, cost for small firms, cyber security, trust, fraud.
  • Example paper: digital systems for food-fraud prevention (olive oil adulteration, NMR, DNA, supply-chain data).

10. Corporate social responsibility and sustainable food systems (electives)

  • Frameworks: ISO 26000, OECD guidelines, UN Global Compact, SDGs, GRI, the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) and European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), German Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (LkSG), packaging rules (PPWR).
  • Value-chain thinking: map the chain from farm to shelf, find material issues (for example sustainable oat sourcing, packaging, climate emissions), set targets, report and verify.
  • Case study: General Mills and the Cheerios oat cereal value chain (company CSR status, legal alignment EU and US, material issues).

11. International organic food markets and marketing

How it was assessed: oral exam; no written paper was submitted, so this section only describes what the module covers.

  • Organic regulation: (EU) 2018/848; certification by approved control bodies; equivalence and compliance schemes for imports.
  • Markets: size and growth of organic markets, distribution channels, price premium, consumer motives (health, environment, animal welfare, taste).
  • Marketing: the marketing mix (4 Ps, 7 Ps), segmentation, targeting and positioning, export strategy.

  • Food security (scientific paper).
  • Consumer behaviour: twelve exercises and final term paper.
